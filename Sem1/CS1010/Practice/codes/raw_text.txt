The old stationmaster at Oakhaven hadn’t seen a train on the tracks in thirty-two years, yet every evening at 8:14 PM, he pulled out his brass pocket watch, adjusted his cap, and stood on the platform with his lantern lit.

The locals thought it was a harmless delusion—a retired man clinging to a bygone era. They’d wave from the road as they drove past in their quiet electric cars, smiling at the old man bathed in yellow lamplight.

Then came a foggy Tuesday in November.

At 8:12 PM, the air grew unusually cold. The quiet hum of the crickets stopped abruptly. By 8:13 PM, a rhythmic, deep vibrations began to shudder through the long-abandoned iron rails, shaking off decades of rust and overgrown ivy.

The stationmaster didn't look surprised. He adjusted his coat against the sudden chill, stepped back behind the yellow line, and raised his lantern high into the mist.

At precisely 8:14 PM, a headlight cut through the fog—bright, white, and silently bearing down on a track that led nowhere.
