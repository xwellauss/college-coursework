Class 1: Paragraph
In written form, English is divided into paragraphs to distinguish one main idea from other main ideas. The paragraph is the basic unit of composition. A paragraph is a group of sentences which develop one central idea. The central idea is usually stated in a topic sentence. Every sentence in the paragraph must help the development of the topic sentence.
Indentation: The first sentence of a paragraph is always indented so that the reader will know that a new subject-or a different aspect of the same subject-is being dealt with. Topic Sentence The first sentence of a paragraph is usually called the topic sentence. It is possible to place the topic sentence at the end of the paragraph (as a kind of conclusion), or even in the middle (as a kind of link between the two parts). It is even possible not to have a topic sentence at all; in this case. we say that the topic sentence is implied or suggested. You should be aware of these possibilities when you read; otherwise, you might miss the point which the author is trying to make.

Class 2: Enumeration
Enumeration is a rhetorical device or technique in writing and speaking where a speaker or writer lists or details the elements, characteristics, reasons, or points of a topic in a systematic manner. It is used to organize information clearly and effectively, often to emphasize the variety or extent of a subject.
Enumerators can be listed through numerals (1, 2, 3…) or listing signals (first, second,…)

Class 3: Gloss and Footnote
Glosses are short explanatory notes placed in the margins or beside the text. Purpose: To define or clarify specific terms, phrases, or cultural references. Immediate clarity: Glosses provide definitions without interrupting reading flow. Vocabulary building: Helps readers learn unfamiliar terms. Cultural and contextual insight: Explains references that may be unfamiliar to readers. Sometimes glosses are also “interlinear,” i.e., written between the lines. Medieval scholars were particularly fond of glosses and it is not uncommon for a page to be more gloss than text in a medieval or early modern book.
Footnores are detailed notes placed at the bottom of a page. Purpose: To provide additional context, citations, or elaboration on specific points. Adds depth: Provides more detailed explanations than glosses. Contextualizes references: Helps readers understand historical, scientific, or literary details. Supports research: Useful for citations and references in academic writing.
