
## Communication Skills Study Guide

The slides cover three units: **Email & Resume**, **Project Writing**, and **Ambiguity**. They are in your syllabus order below.

### Unit 1: Email and Resume

#### Emails

**Purpose:** Requests, inquiries, updates, or general correspondence. Clear emails show professionalism, build relationships, and prevent misunderstandings.

|Type|Use|Example from slides|
|---|---|---|
|Professional|Work-related|“Dear Mr. Smith, I hope you are doing well. I wanted to schedule a meeting...”|
|Informal|Friends and acquaintances, casual|“Hey Sarah, I was thinking about our trip next week!”|

**Etiquette checklist:**

1. **Concise and clear:** “Please confirm receipt of this email.”
2. **No overuse of capitals:** “Please send the report” vs. “PLEASE SEND THE REPORT!!!”
3. **Proofread:** no typos or grammar errors.
4. **Correct tone** for the audience.
5. **Subject line:** a blank one confuses the recipient.
6. **Attachments:** double-check before sending.

**Bad email examples on the slides:**

- “Hey Dr. Smith... you was telling us... ASAP? Thanks!!!” is too casual, has a grammar error, and has a vague subject (“Assignment”).
- “Dear John, Hi, i am student of... im interested...” has a vague subject (“Project”), lowercase “i”/”im”, a mixed greeting, and rambling wording.

**Exercise formula (formal inquiry email, 120-150 words):**  
formal salutation → how you found the program → at least 3 specific questions (deadline, qualifications, structure) → polite closing → “Yours sincerely.”

#### Resumes

**Purpose:** A personal marketing document. It highlights qualifications, experience, and skills, and it is your first introduction to an employer.

|Type|What it does|Best for|Slide example|
|---|---|---|---|
|**Chronological**|Work history in reverse order|Consistent experience|5 years in marketing (James Applicant)|
|**Functional**|Skills and experience over history|Career gaps|Education → HR (Jose Applicant)|
|**Combination**|Skills plus chronological history|Unrelated jobs or gaps, when the employer wants work history|Joe Applicant|

**Tailoring a resume:**

- Emphasize skills relevant to the job (e.g. social media, SEO, and market analysis for marketing).
- Read the job description and match your skills to it.
- Keep it clean, readable, and to the point, with bullet points for clarity.
- **Avoid:** typos, long paragraphs, outdated info, and “References available upon request” (it’s implied).

### Unit 2: Project Writing

**Purpose:** Present findings, processes, and outcomes clearly and in an organized way. It is crucial for sharing research, proposals, or analysis with stakeholders, and for project tracking and evaluation.

#### Components of a project report (memorize the order)

|#|Section|Content|
|---|---|---|
|1|Title Page|Title, team members, date, other details|
|2|Abstract|Brief summary of goals, methods, results, conclusions (**150-200 words**)|
|3|Table of Contents|Outline of all sections|
|4|Introduction|Overview of problem, goals, relevance|
|5|Methodology|How the project was conducted|
|6|Results|Key findings|
|7|Discussion|**Interpretation** of results|
|8|Conclusion|What was learned, plus recommendations|

#### Section details

- **Introduction:** sets the stage with context, problem, and objectives. Its components are background information, problem definition, objectives, and scope of the study.  
    _Example: “This project explores the influence of digital advertisements on the purchasing habits of consumers aged 18-34...”_
- **Methodology:** explains how the work was done, specific enough that someone else could **replicate** it. Its components are research design (survey, interviews, experiment), sampling methods, tools and materials, and data collection and analysis techniques.  
    _Example: “We conducted a survey of 500 participants aged 18-34...”_
- **Results:** presents findings **without interpretation**, using tables, charts, graphs, or statistics, with a clear and concise description.  
    _Example: “Out of the 500 respondents, 65% reported that they had purchased a product after seeing an online ad.”_

#### Common mistakes

|Mistake|Fix|
|---|---|
|Vague descriptions of methods|Detailed, step-by-step descriptions|
|Overloading Results with unnecessary info|Only the most relevant data, presented clearly|
|Weak or unsupported conclusions|Conclusions directly supported by the data|

#### Class task: “Employee Productivity” project

Likely exam material is a sample Results section for the given data (70% more productive, 25% less productive, 5% no change). Here is a model:

> _Of the respondents, 70% reported increased productivity while working remotely. A further 25% felt less productive, citing distractions at home, while the remaining 5% showed no significant change in productivity._

Notice that it states the numbers only and does not explain why or recommend anything. That belongs in Discussion and Conclusion.

### Unit 3: Ambiguity

**Definition:** Two or more possible meanings in a single word, phrase, or sentence. It leads to confusion.

**Why it matters:** Misunderstandings in conversation, errors in professional or legal contexts, and difficulty interpreting written instructions.

**Real-world consequences:**

- **Legal:** ambiguous contract language causes disputes about terms, so legal documents are carefully worded.
- **Business:** an email subject like “Meeting tomorrow” could be a reminder or a request to schedule.

#### The 5 types

|Type|Definition|Examples and fixes|
|---|---|---|
|**Lexical**|A word has more than one meaning; context decides|“I went to the bank.” / “She can’t bear children” (unable to have them, or finds them intolerable). Fix: “She is unable to have children due to medical reasons.”|
|**Syntactic (Structural)**|Sentence structure or grammar allows more than one reading|“Visiting relatives can be annoying” (the act of visiting, or relatives who visit).|
|**Semantic**|Meaning unclear due to vague language or multiple interpretations of a term|“Every student read a book” (the same book, or different books?). “He gave her cat food” → “He gave food to her cat” / “He gave her some cat food.”|
|**Pragmatic**|Meaning unclear due to context, culture, or situation|“Can you pass the salt?” (request or question about ability?) → “Please pass the salt.” / “Are you able to pass the salt?”|
|**Phonological**|Spoken sounds are identical or similar|Homophones: here/hear, tail/tale, knight/night, break/brake. Boundaries: “I scream” / “ice cream”; “The stuffy nose” / “The stuff he knows.” Grammar: “Your students” / “You’re students.”|

**Phonemes note:** phonemes are sounds, not letters. _Cat_ has 3 letters and 3 phonemes (/k/ /a/ /t/). _Ship_ has 4 letters but 3 phonemes (/sh/ /i/ /p/). Phonological ambiguity mostly occurs in speech because listeners lack spelling and punctuation cues.

**Other slide example:** “You’re looking hot today” (physically warm, or complimenting appearance?). Unambiguous: “You’re looking very attractive today.” / “You seem warm today.”

#### Strategies to resolve ambiguity

1. **Use specific words:** “He’s going to the bank” → “He’s going to the financial bank.”
2. **Rearrange sentence structure:** “She saw the man with the binoculars” → “Using binoculars, she saw the man.”
3. **Add context or explanation:** give extra detail to prevent misunderstanding.

#### Class activity answers

|Sentence|Meaning A|Meaning B|
|---|---|---|
|“They are hunting dogs.”|They are dogs bred for hunting.|They are people hunting for dogs.|
|“The chicken is ready to eat.”|The cooked chicken is ready to be eaten.|The chicken is hungry and ready to eat.|
|“We saw her duck.”|We saw the duck belonging to her.|We saw her lower her head.|

#### The Emily and Claire paragraph

The ambiguous version uses “she/her” and “it” so often that you can’t tell who is who. The fix is to **replace pronouns with names or nouns**:

- “She told her...” → “Emily told Claire...”
- “the painting she had submitted with her colleague” → “the painting Emily had co-created with her colleague”
- “a similar work” → “another artist’s work”
- “the other gallery” → “the rival gallery”
- “That was an extraordinary piece of work” → “The sculpture is an extraordinary piece of work.”
