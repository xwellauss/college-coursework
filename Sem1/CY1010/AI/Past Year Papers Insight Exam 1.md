## 1. Calculation Templates (these recur every year — practice the METHOD, not just the answer)

### A) ppm ↔ Molarity conversion

**ppm (mg/L) = Molarity (mol/L) × Molar mass (g/mol) × 1000**

> Example: 560 mM Cl⁻ (molar mass 35.45 g/mol) → 0.560 × 35.45 × 1000 ≈ **19,900 ppm**

### B) Steam Methane Reforming stoichiometry

CH₄ + H₂O → CO + 3H₂

- Convert grams of CH₄ → moles (÷16 g/mol)
- Multiply by 3 (mole ratio)
- Convert back to grams using H₂'s molar mass = **2 g/mol** (not 1!)

> Example: 32 g CH₄ = 2 mol → 6 mol H₂ → **12 g H₂**

### C) Global energy consumption → Watts

**Power (W) = Energy (J) / Time (s)**

- 1 EJ = 10¹⁸ J; 1 year ≈ 3.15×10⁷ s

> Example: 500 EJ/year = 500×10¹⁸ J ÷ 3.15×10⁷ s ≈ **1.585×10¹³ W**

### D) Wind power scaling

**Power ∝ v³** (cube of wind velocity, not linear!)

> If velocity doubles (10→20 km/h): power increases **2³ = 8-fold**

### E) Surface area-to-volume ratio scaling (particles)

For a sphere: SA ∝ r², Volume ∝ r³, so **SA/V ∝ 1/r**

> If particle size (radius) shrinks by a factor of 10, SA/V ratio increases by a factor of **10** (linear — not squared or cubed, easy trap).

### F) Atom Economy — full worked example

**Atom Economy (%) = (Mass of desired product / Total mass of all reactants) × 100**

> Reaction: CH₃CH₂Br + NH₃ → CH₃CH₂NH₂ (desired) + HBr Molar masses: CH₃CH₂Br = 108.97, NH₃ = 17.03, CH₃CH₂NH₂ = 45.08 Atom economy = 45.08 / (108.97 + 17.03) × 100 ≈ **35.8% (~36.6%)** _(Same formula as your existing green chemistry metrics — just make sure you can plug in numbers under exam time pressure.)_

### G) Beta-oxidation math

For a saturated, even-numbered fatty acid with **n carbons**:

- Acetyl-CoA produced = **n/2**
- Number of beta-oxidation cycles = **(n/2) − 1**

For odd numbered: (n-3)/2 cycles and ACoA produced.

> Palmitic acid (C16) → **8 acetyl-CoA**, over **7 cycles** Odd-chain fatty acids: the final leftover propionyl-CoA is converted to **succinyl-CoA**, which enters the TCA/Krebs cycle (not converted to glucose, not excreted).

## 2. Biochemistry / Xenobiotics / Enzyme Kinetics (appears on Exam 1 despite being light in the lecture slides — worth knowing cold)

**Krebs (Citric Acid) Cycle**

- It is an **8-step process** (not 4, 6, or 10)
- Acetyl-CoA + **Oxaloacetate** → Citrate (first combining molecule)
- **Aconitase** converts citrate → isocitrate
- α-ketoglutarate dehydrogenase acts further down the cycle

**Protein basics**

- Monomeric unit of protein = **Amino acid**
- Beta-oxidation in eukaryotic cells occurs in the **mitochondrial matrix**

**Xenobiotics**

- Definition: foreign compounds **not naturally produced by the body**
- Primary metabolizing organ: **Liver**
- Phase I reactions: **hydrolysis, oxidation, and reduction**
- Main goal of xenobiotic metabolism: **increase water solubility for excretion** (not to produce energy, not to store in fat)

**Enzyme Kinetics — Lineweaver-Burk plot**

- Slope = Km/Vmax; Y-intercept = 1/Vmax
- Main use: **identifying the type of enzyme inhibition** (not measuring ATP, not sequencing)
- **Competitive inhibition** → No change in y-intercept (Vmax unchanged); slope changes (apparent Km increases)
- **Non-competitive inhibition** → inhibitor can bind **both** the free enzyme and the enzyme-substrate complex

**Arrhenius equation limitation**

- Fails to accurately describe protein/enzyme reaction rates at **extremely high temperatures** (denaturation breaks the assumption of a stable active site)

## 3. Persistent Organic Pollutants / "Dirty Dozen" (shows up on Exam 1, not just Exam 2)

- "Dirty Dozen" = **12 persistent organic pollutants (POPs)**
- International agreement to restrict them: **Stockholm Convention**
- Defining characteristic: they **persist in the environment and bioaccumulate**
- CO₂ is explicitly **NOT** one of the original Dirty Dozen POPs (common distractor)
- A chemical is considered "highly persistent" if its half-life in sediment is **> 6 months**
- **PBT** = **P**ersistent, **B**ioaccumulative, and **T**oxic
- ⚠️ **Flag to double-check with your instructor:** Minamata disease is scientifically caused by **methylmercury** poisoning (industrial wastewater contamination in Minamata, Japan) — one past paper's marked key showed a different answer (PCBs). Go with methylmercury unless your specific lecture slide says otherwise.

### Direct Air Capture — full solvent regeneration loop (new detail)

The DAC solvent (causticization) cycle has **two linked regeneration steps, both correct together**:

1. KOH captures CO₂ → forms K₂CO₃; K₂CO₃ + Ca(OH)₂ → CaCO₃↓ + **regenerates KOH**
2. CaCO₃ is calcined (heated) → CaO + CO₂; **CaO + H₂O → regenerates Ca(OH)₂** for the next cycle.
3. Amine-functionalized MOFs capture CO₂ via **both chemisorption (amine-CO₂ carbamate formation) and physisorption (framework pore adsorption)** — not one or the other.

### Biofuel/Biodiesel clarifications

- Second-generation biofuel source example: **rice husk** (a lignocellulosic agricultural residue)
- Biodiesel can be produced from: **algae, waste cooking oil, or fat — all of these**
- Biodiesel transesterification substrate: **lipids AND algae (both)**
- Bioethanol is produced via fermentation of: **starch** (not lignocellulose directly — lignocellulose needs pretreatment/hydrolysis first)
- Best plastic waste for **graphene synthesis**: **polyolefins**
- Best plastic to produce **jet fuel**: **polyolefins**

### Plastics — key statistics (frequently tested, memorize these numbers)

- From 1950–2015, virgin plastic production = 8.3 billion metric tons; of that, only **~2.5 billion metric tons is still in use** (the rest already discarded)
- At current generation rates (~400 Mt/year), cumulative plastic waste by 2050 is projected to reach **~25,000 Mt**
- **79%** of all plastic waste ever produced remains in landfills/the environment (only ~9% recycled, ~12% incinerated) — this is the single most-cited plastic statistic, know it cold
- Algal bloom formation in lakes attributable to detergent use: **~25%**
- Monomer molecular mass range: **10–100 Da** (much smaller than polymers, which run 10,000–100,000+ Da)
- Polyethylene is the polymer most **quickly discarded as waste** (single-use packaging)
- Plastic degradation follows **pseudo-zero-order kinetics**; half-life = **ratio of amount of plastic to the rate constant** (t½ = [A]₀/2k — the defining zero-order half-life formula)
- Recycling codes: **Type 5 plastic (PP) is actually harder to recycle than Type 1 (PET)** — a common false-statement trap
- Least preferred method to reduce plastic pollution (vs reduce/reuse): **incineration**
- Function of **scrubbers** in incinerators: regulate particulate matter (distinct from electrostatic precipitators, which do similar but via different mechanism)

|---|---|

### Pesticides / POPs / Polymers — specific structural facts

- **Lindane** is used as an **insecticide** (organochlorine)
- Tire rubber is made from **styrene-butadiene** (SBR), not latex/cellulose/nylon
- **Nicotine sulfate** is the odd one out — it is **not** a synthetic pesticide (naturally derived from tobacco), unlike endosulfan/PFOS/benzoyl sulfonate
- **DDT has 5 carbon-chlorine bonds** (2 aromatic C-Cl from the two chlorophenyl rings + 3 from the -CCl₃ group)
- **Phenanthrene** (a PAH) is the one compound among dioxins/endrin/dieldrin/phenanthrene that does **NOT** contain carbon-chlorine bonds
- **Furans** (polychlorinated dibenzofurans) have **no intentional use** — they are unwanted toxic byproducts, like dioxins
- Chemical produced by burning chlorine-containing medical/municipal waste: **Tetrachlorodibenzo-p-dioxin (TCDD)**

### Biopolymer / physical chemistry odds and ends

- Structure/function of biopolymers is attributed to **quaternary structure**
- A **large Henry's Law constant (K_H)** for a chemical indicates a strong tendency toward **evaporation from water** (high volatility, low water affinity)
- Electron activity (**pE**) of water around 25 favors **oxidation** (high pE = oxidizing conditions)
- **poly(ε-caprolactone)** is synthesized via **chain polymerization** (specifically ring-opening polymerization, which is chain-growth mechanism)
- **BOD (Biochemical Oxygen Demand)**, not COD, gives the more accurate estimate of _biodegradable_ organic pollutant load in water (COD measures total chemically-oxidizable material, including non-biodegradable)
